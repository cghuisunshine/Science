Part B — Short Answer Practice

Write your answer first. Then open the model answer to check your understanding.

1. What is the universe?
The universe includes all matter, energy, and space. It contains galaxies, stars, planets, moons, gas, dust, asteroids, comets, and a huge amount of empty space.
2. What is the difference between a galaxy and a solar system?
A galaxy is a huge collection of stars, gas, and dust held together by gravity. A solar system is a star and the objects that orbit it, such as planets, moons, asteroids, and comets.
3. Why is the Milky Way important to us?
The Milky Way is important because it is our home galaxy. Our solar system is located inside the Milky Way.
4. What is a light-year?
A light-year is a distance, not a time. It is the distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 trillion kilometres.
5. What does the Big Bang theory say?
The Big Bang theory says that the universe began from a very hot, dense state and then expanded. Over time, matter formed particles, then atoms, stars, and galaxies.
6. Explain the Doppler effect using a siren example.
When an ambulance moves toward you, the sound waves are squeezed together, so the pitch sounds higher. When it moves away, the waves stretch out, so the pitch sounds lower. This is the Doppler effect.
7. What is red-shift, and what does it tell scientists?
Red-shift happens when light waves are stretched toward the red end of the spectrum. It tells scientists that an object, such as a distant galaxy, is moving away from us.
8. What is blue-shift?
Blue-shift happens when light waves are squeezed toward the blue end of the spectrum. It shows that an object is moving toward us.
9. What is CMBR?
CMBR stands for Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation. It is faint microwave radiation found throughout the universe and is considered leftover energy, or the afterglow, from the Big Bang.
10. Give two main pieces of evidence for the Big Bang theory.
Two main pieces of evidence are: (1) the red-shift of distant galaxies, showing the universe is expanding, and (2) the detection of Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation, which is leftover energy from the early universe.

Part C — Challenge Review

Why does red-shift support the idea of an expanding universe?
If light from most distant galaxies is red-shifted, it means those galaxies are moving away from us. This suggests that space is expanding and carrying galaxies farther apart.
Why is the phrase “Big Bang” a little misleading?
It can sound like an explosion in empty space, but the theory describes the expansion of space itself. Space, time, matter, and energy all began changing from an early hot, dense state.
Compare interplanetary, interstellar, and intergalactic distances.
Interplanetary distances are between planets and are often measured in kilometres or AU. Interstellar distances are between stars in a galaxy and are often measured in light-years. Intergalactic distances are between galaxies and are also measured in light-years.
