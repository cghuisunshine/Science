Pedigrees & Inheritance – Hemophilia in Queen Victoria’s Family

Pedigrees and inheritance: A pedigree in genetics is a diagram showing the lineage (family relationships) of an individual and direct ancestors, used to analyze how a trait is inherited.

Hemophilia has been called a “royal disease” because the hemophilia gene was passed from Queen Victoria (Queen of England in 1837) into the ruling families of Spain, Germany, and Russia. Queen Victoria’s hemophilia allele was caused by a spontaneous mutation.

Hemophilia is inherited in a sex-linked recessive pattern. The genes associated with this condition are located on the X chromosome.

  • Males (XY) have only one X chromosome, so one altered copy of the gene can cause the condition.
  • Females (XX) have two X chromosomes, so they would usually need two altered copies to be affected. This makes hemophilia rare in females.
  • Fathers cannot pass an X-linked trait to their sons (sons inherit the Y from the father).
